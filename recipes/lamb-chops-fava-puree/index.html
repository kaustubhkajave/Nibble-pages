Lamb Chops & Fava Purée

Garlic and oregano lamb chops on a golden split pea fava, with blistered tomatoes, feta, red onion and mint oil.

Recipe by Nibble

Ingredients Cooking steps Plating photos
Total
50 minutes
Serves
2

Ingredients

  • 4–6 lamb rib chops, about 1 lb (450 g) total
  • 1/2 cup (100 g) yellow split peas, rinsed
  • 2 cups (480 ml) water, plus more as needed
  • 6 cherry tomatoes
  • Small bunch of mint (about 1/2 cup / 10 g)
  • 1/4 cup (35 g) crumbled feta
  • 1/2 red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 lemon
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 7 tbsp olive oil, divided
  • 1/2 tsp dried oregano
  • 3/4 tsp salt, divided
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper

Cooking steps

  1. 01

    Simmer the split peas with 1 garlic clove in the water for 30–35 minutes until very soft, skimming off any foam and adding water if it gets dry. Blend with 3 tablespoons oil, the juice of half the lemon, 1/4 teaspoon salt and a splash of water until smooth. Keep warm.

  2. 02

    Blend the mint with 3 tablespoons oil and a pinch of salt, then strain. Soak the onion in cold water with a squeeze of lemon.

  3. 03

    Rub the chops with 1 teaspoon oil, the remaining garlic clove (grated), the oregano, the remaining salt and the pepper.

  4. 04

    Heat a skillet with the remaining oil over medium-high heat. Sear the chops for 2–3 minutes per side, until the center reaches 145°F (63°C). Rest for at least 3 minutes.

  5. 05

    Blister the tomatoes in the same pan for about 3 minutes.

Plate it up

  1. Step 1

    Golden fava purée

    Spoon about 4 tablespoons of fava toward the upper left of each plate and smooth it into a thick curved swoosh.

  2. Step 2

    Seared lamb chops

    Lean 2 or 3 lamb chops against each other on the fava, bones pointing up.

  3. Step 3

    Blistered tomatoes

    Place 3 blistered tomatoes along the right base of the chops.

  4. Step 4

    Feta and red onion

    Scatter 2 tablespoons of feta and a few onion slices along the left side of the chops.

  5. Step 5

    Mint oil

    Spoon a little mint oil over the chops, letting a few drops fall onto the fava.
